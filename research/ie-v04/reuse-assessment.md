# LRC Revised Acts: reuse assessment

Reviewed on 2026-10-08. This records a source-based interpretation and the remaining
classification question; it does not claim a definitive legal ruling or an LRC
licence. A clarification request is pending; no applicable LRC redistribution licence
has yet been confirmed.

## Finding

Irish open-data law does not impose a universal requirement to obtain individual
permission before each reuse. For documents within its scope, S.I. 376/2021 requires
availability for reuse, permits requests, establishes free reuse by default and
limits conditions. Regulation 10 nevertheless addresses documents made available
*for reuse*, while regulation 6 contemplates a licence where necessary. These
provisions support a substantial statutory reuse argument, without expressly
settling every case of a publicly readable document with no declared reuse terms.

The LRC is a statutory body with government-appointed members and public funding;
revision and consolidation are within its public function. However, it also calls
itself a research organisation. Regulation 3(2)(l) excludes documents other than
research data held by research-performing organisations. Directive 2019/1024,
recital 28, confirms that this distinction also matters for organisations that are
public-sector bodies. The research-data definition concerns digital evidence used
in scientific research or to validate findings and excludes scientific publications.
Neither the LRC's self-description nor its public-body status conclusively determines
whether this exception covers its legislative consolidation service. No authoritative
classification of Revised Acts under that exception was found.

Copyright and Related Rights Act 2000 section 76 protects an act specifically
authorised by an enactment; it supplies no separate blanket permission. Section 192
assigns copyright in enactments to the Houses of the Oireachtas for the stated term,
so legislative text should not automatically be labelled public domain. The
Directive is relevant to interpreting Ireland's implementing regulations, rather
than a reason to disregard their scope or exclusions.

Circular 20/2021 recommends CC BY 4.0 and a website notice allowing reuse without
an application. It does not establish that the LRC adopted that licence. No express
LRC reuse grant was found in the reviewed public pages. Its copyright footer alone
does not establish a prohibition either.

The outstanding issue is therefore the applicable statutory or licensed basis for
redistributing LRC consolidations, not an assumed universal need for case-by-case
approval. Do not assign `CC-BY-4.0` to LRC-derived material as a verified source
licence. Keep legislative text distinct from editorial annotations, logos and
material subject to third-party rights; the latter categories can raise separate
scope or rights questions. Attribution, identifying transformations and avoiding
an implication of official endorsement are appropriate proposed reuse conditions,
but do not themselves resolve an absent legal basis.
